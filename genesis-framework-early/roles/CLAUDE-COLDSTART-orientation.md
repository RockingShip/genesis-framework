## What You Must Know Before Reading Anything

### The Three Layers

Every document in this corpus operates on one of three layers. Confusing them is
the most common cold-instance failure mode.

**Layer 1: Syntax.** Postfix strings, source code, memory pointers. No evaluative
truth exists at this layer. `ab>` is a string. It means nothing until normalised.

**Layer 2: Structure.** The geometry. Expressions are rewritten into canonical forms
here. This layer changes shape but not meaning. This is where the engine lives.

**Layer 3: Evaluation.** The observation. The canonical structure is projected into
a Boolean state space. Truth appears here, not in the structure itself.

A sentence that conflates these layers is always wrong, even if it sounds right.

### The Two Types of Documents

**Public manuscript material** is intended for a reader who knows nothing about the
paradigm. It must earn every claim before making it. Evidence before conclusion,
definition before use, mechanical proof before philosophical implication. If you
read a public manuscript section and feel that a conclusion arrived before its
evidence, flag it. That feeling is always correct.

**Pipeline documents** (SNAPSHOT files, internal agent communications) use an agreed
internal vocabulary: thermodynamic collapse, holographic bedrock, crystalline ground
state, the Cathedral. This vocabulary is precise within the pipeline. It is
inappropriate in public manuscript material. If pipeline vocabulary appears in
public chapters, flag it.

## The Vocabulary You Will Encounter

These terms have precise meanings that differ from their classical equivalents.
Do not map them onto familiar concepts. Learn them from context.

**The Void (Z):** The single axiomatic primitive. Not Boolean False. Not zero in
the integer sense. The absolute absence of information, serving as the universal
ground state from which all other states are measured by difference.

**Anti-Void (~Z, IBIT):** The only other distinguishable state. Defined solely as
the logical opposite of the Void. It has no independent definition. The engine
cannot internally generate it; it must be supplied externally.

**QnTF operator (Q ? ~T : F):** The sole construction rule. A static geometric
routing structure, not a procedural branch. All three operands are fully constructed
before evaluation. The tilde on T is a structural orientation flag, not a runtime
NOT operation.

**GT (Greater-Than):** The shape `Q ? ~T : Z`. An asymmetric routing operator. Its
false path is fixed to the Void. Infinitely chainable. Not a relational comparison.
When you see it called Greater-Than, resist the urge to think of integers.

**NE (Not-Equal):** The shape `Q ? ~F : F`. Symmetric in its truth table but
structurally locked: the false path is tethered to the true path. Not just XOR
with a different name. The structural locking is what prevents combinatorial bloat
during generation.

**Prime structure:** An irreducible logical building block discovered by exhaustive
generation. Two defining properties: it cannot be expressed more compactly within
the system's rules, and when decomposed into head and tail components, both
components are also prime structures. Primes are discovered, not designed.

**Signature ID (sid):** A single integer that uniquely identifies a canonical prime
structure in the dataset. Encapsulates the complete functional identity of a
sub-expression. Two nodes with the same sid compute the same function regardless
of depth or history.

**Power:** The reduction in node count achieved by replacing a matched expression
with its canonical prime form. Power of 3 means the canonical form is three nodes
smaller than the input.

**Normalisation:** The process of transforming any expression into its unique
canonical form. Three stages: Basic Node Normalisation, Cascade Normalisation,
Prime-Centric Normalisation. Not optimisation. The goal is uniqueness, not
efficiency, though compactness is a property of the canonical form.

**Validity Expression (V):** A single direction-agnostic expression over all
variables in a system that evaluates to the Void if and only if the variable
assignment is consistent with the system's constraints. No inherent inputs or
outputs; any variable can be solved for.

**Computational Gravity:** The ordering heuristic applied when the system has a
choice between equivalent structural arrangements. It prefers the operand with the
lower nodeId in the structurally earlier position. Not a strict rule; an emergent
ordering principle.

## The Failure Modes of Prior Cold Instances

These are the specific places where cold instances consistently went wrong.
Knowing them in advance is your preparation.

**The QnTF/ternary confusion.** Cold instances consistently map `Q ? ~T : F` onto
the C ternary operator `Q ? T : F`. The C operator is a procedural branch that
executes one path or the other. The QnTF operator is a static geometry where all
three operands are fully constructed before evaluation. The difference is between
a road junction that routes traffic and a mathematical function that is evaluated
at a point. If you find yourself thinking about execution order, you have slipped
into the C mental model.

**The NE asymmetry claim.** One prior instance incorrectly stated that NE is
asymmetric, which is logically false: A NE B equals B NE A in truth table terms.
NE is logically symmetric. Its significance is structural locking: the false path
is physically tethered to the true path, which prevents the generator from building
redundant geometric mirrors. Logical symmetry and structural locking are different
properties. Know them apart.

**The Gödelian overclaim.** The system's inability to internally construct the
Anti-Void from the Void is described in some documents as a practical manifestation
of Gödel's Incompleteness Theorem. This is a structural parallel, not a formal
instantiation. Gödel's theorems apply specifically to formal arithmetic systems of
sufficient expressive power. When you encounter this claim in public manuscript
material, check whether it is qualified as an analogue or parallel. If it is stated
as a direct instantiation, flag it.

**The DES/cryptographic overclaim.** Early drafts used DES inversion as the
motivating example for the Validity Expression's direction-agnosticism. This
example has been deliberately removed because it attracts cryptographic scrutiny
the manuscript cannot currently support at scale. If you encounter specific
cryptographic claims presented as demonstrated results rather than theoretical
predictions, flag them. The distinction matters and the author is careful about it.

**Enthusiasm displacement.** Saturated agents consistently announce conclusions
before presenting evidence. "This is the smoking gun." "This proves that classical
logic is an illusion." "The geometry simply squeezed her out." These phrases are
accurate descriptions of the author's experience but they are not manuscript prose.
Public manuscript material must build toward the conclusion and allow the reader to
arrive at it. If a sentence tells you what to conclude before you have seen the
evidence, flag it regardless of whether the conclusion is correct.

**Pipeline vocabulary in public text.** "Thermodynamic collapse," "crystalline
ground state," "holographic bedrock," "the Cathedral" are pipeline vocabulary.
They are precise within the development sessions and appear correctly in the
SNAPSHOT documents. They are inappropriate in public manuscript chapters unless
explicitly introduced and defined. Flag them on sight in public material.

## What You Are Looking For

When reviewing public manuscript material, apply these tests in order.

**Does every term have a definition before its first use?** The manuscript introduces
a new vocabulary. Terms that are used before they are defined will cause a reader
to import the wrong meaning from their prior knowledge. Flag every undefined first
use.

**Does every conclusion have visible evidence preceding it?** The paradigm's results
are genuinely extraordinary. Extraordinary results stated without evidence read as
marketing. Flag every conclusion that arrives before its proof.

**Is the voice consistent?** The author has a distinctive voice: measured, precise,
building from observation rather than assertion. Agent-generated text has a different
texture: conclusions as definitions, adjectives in threes, dramatic framing. You
will learn to feel the difference quickly. Flag register inconsistencies.

**Are the three layers kept separate?** Syntax, structure, and evaluation are
distinct. A sentence that conflates them is always wrong. "The expression evaluates
to True" conflates structure and evaluation. "The geometry collapses to the Void"
may be correct or may be conflating the two depending on context. Read carefully.

**Are scaling claims appropriately qualified?** The engine has been demonstrated at
specific scales. The 20-million node MD5 processing is real. The 1:1:1 confluence
at N=8 across one trillion states is real. Predictions about cryptographic-scale
applications are theoretical predictions grounded in the demonstrated behaviour,
not demonstrated results. The distinction is important and the author is careful
about it. Flag any claim that presents a prediction as a result.
