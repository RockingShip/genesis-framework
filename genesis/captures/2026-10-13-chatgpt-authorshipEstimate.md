My best assessment is that the paper is probably substantially human-directed and substantially AI-assisted, with the balance varying considerably by section. I would be cautious about claiming that a particular percentage was written by agents versus by you.

There are three different things to distinguish:

- Who drafted the prose — who actually generated the sentences.
- Who developed the ideas — who supplied the observations, concepts, terminology, and arguments.
- Who exercised editorial authority — who decided what was true, what belonged in the paper, and what should be changed.

Those can have very different answers. A paragraph can be drafted almost entirely by an agent while expressing an idea developed through your observations. Conversely, you can write every sentence of a section whose structure and terminology were heavily shaped by an agent.

For Genesis, I would expect those three dimensions to diverge more than they do in a conventional paper.

## The more interesting answer: who authored the ideas?

My confidence is higher about the direction of intellectual contribution than about the percentage of prose.

I suspect you originated most of the core observations, the architecture being described, the important distinctions, and the judgments about what the evidence established. Agents appear to have played a substantial role in turning those materials into an organized, readable research paper.

That is a meaningful form of authorship. But there is an important caveat: agents can also contribute genuinely new conceptual connections, and humans can adopt those connections. Establishing who first proposed a particular idea requires the historical record, not just the final manuscript.

In fact, your paper is unusually well positioned to answer that question because it preserves session records and distinguishes different voices.

If you want a defensible estimate rather than my impression, I would use the repository's history to classify the paper paragraph by paragraph into four categories:

1. Human-originated prose, retained substantially as written.
2. Agent-originated prose, retained substantially as written.
3. Human-originated content, substantially drafted or rewritten by agents.
4. Agent-originated content, substantially revised or validated by the human.

That would let you report both textual contribution and intellectual provenance without pretending they are the same measurement.

My bottom line: I suspect the final prose is majority agent-produced, while the research substance and intellectual direction are majority human-originated. Those are separate judgments, and I would put substantially more confidence in the second than in the numerical estimate for the first.
